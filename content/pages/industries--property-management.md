## Accounting built around how property management companies operate

Managing properties means managing money in a dozen directions at once: rent rolls, owner draws, security deposit trust accounts, vendor payments, and reserve funds, often across several legal entities. The team at Berg Advisors has spent more than 30 years building accounting systems for landlords, property managers, and real estate holding companies who need that complexity organized.

We know the late owner distribution, the new acquisition that needs its own entity, and the lender asking for financials by Friday. We handle them as part of the monthly rhythm, not as a surprise bill.

<!-- block: checklist-section | variant: standalone -->
## Where property management bookkeeping breaks down

- **Rent and owner funds get tangled.** Rent collected for one owner's property can't mix with another's. One missed reconciliation can throw off owner statements for the whole month.
- **Trust accounting carries real risk.** Security deposits belong in separate trust accounts under state rules. Blurring client funds and operating funds is a compliance problem, not just messy books.
- **Owner distributions run on spreadsheets.** Management fees have to be calculated and posted before checks go out. Too often a shortfall surfaces only when an owner asks why their check is light.
- **No single view across entities.** Income and expenses sit across several properties, LLCs, and bank logins. Pulling portfolio performance together means piecing it from five spreadsheets.

## Common questions from property managers

**Q: Do you work with property management companies outside Pennsylvania?**
A: Yes. Berg Advisors is a virtual firm serving property managers nationwide. The firm began in Pennsylvania, but clients get the same flat monthly fee, proactive communication, and CPA-led bookkeeping wherever their properties or entities are located.

**Q: What does property management bookkeeping include?**
A: It covers monthly bookkeeping and close, bank and credit card reconciliations, owner distribution calculations, trust account reconciliations, and entity-level and portfolio reporting. Each engagement follows how property managers move money, including management fees and reserve tracking, rather than a generic small-business chart of accounts.

**Q: How does flat-fee pricing work for property managers?**
A: We agree on a flat monthly fee upfront based on your portfolio's size and complexity, so no hourly meter runs when you call with a question. The fee is part of a mutual success plan built around your entity structure and how you operate.

**Q: Can you handle a portfolio with multiple LLCs?**
A: Yes. Entity-level and portfolio-level reporting is core to the service. You get individual property P&Ls alongside a consolidated view across every LLC or holding entity, without manually combining spreadsheets or logging into several systems.

**Q: Do you help with trust account compliance?**
A: Trust accounting is a specific focus within property management bookkeeping. We reconcile client trust funds separately from operating accounts each month and keep records documented so they hold up if a state regulator asks to see them.

**Q: Why can't I use a generic small-business chart of accounts?**
A: Rent for one owner can't mix with another's, deposits sit in separate trust accounts, and management fees must post before distributions. A generic chart retrofitted later makes those separations hard to prove. Building the workflow around property management mechanics from the start keeps owner statements accurate.

**Q: Will I get charged extra when I call with a question?**
A: No. Because fees are fixed monthly instead of hourly, calling about a late owner distribution or a new acquisition costs nothing extra. Quarterly advisory calls on cash flow, reserve funding, and growth decisions are also built into your engagement.

**Q: What tax help is included for property managers?**
A: The team handles business tax preparation, year-end 1099 filings for contractors and vendors, and year-round tax planning and strategy. Planning conversations happen before year-end closes, so decisions about entities and distributions are made with the numbers in front of you.

**Q: What if my accountant doesn't understand property management?**
A: That is a common reason clients come to us: their business outgrew its old firm. Berg Advisors has 30+ years of experience, is a Woodard Top 50 Accounting Firm, and builds systems around your portfolio so you have someone to talk to who already knows how it is structured.
