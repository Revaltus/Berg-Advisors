## LinkedIn

A seller orders 5,000 units at $8. By the time freight, customs, and warehouse prep are paid, she has spent $44,500 before a single unit sells.

On the P&L, nothing has happened yet. Under accrual accounting, inventory is an asset until the unit sells, so the cost only reaches COGS as units leave. Her first 1,000 units show $24,000 in revenue against about $9,000 of COGS. Meanwhile roughly $35,000 of cash is still sitting on shelves, and payroll is due.

That gap is the cash conversion cycle, and for many product sellers it runs 120 to 200 days. Growth makes it worse, because the bigger order has to be paid for before the extra revenue exists.

The post covers what monthly accrual books show you by SKU, the five questions worth answering before any large purchase order, and how a 13-week cash forecast turns reorders from guesswork into a plan. If you sell across Amazon, Shopify, Walmart, or eBay, it is worth a read.
